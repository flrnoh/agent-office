# Features

Everything in the office, room by room. Back to the [README](../README.md).

- **A floor per project.** The first time the office starts in a terminal it walks you through picking your first project (see [Run locally](../README.md#run-locally)). Otherwise you start inside the elevator, and it asks for one: pick one of the repositories your `gh` login can see (or type `owner/name`) and the office clones it into the workspace folder, `~/agent-office/<owner>/<repo>` unless you pick another (**📁 Change folder** right there, for admins). While it clones, its floor on the panel says how far along it is (*Downloading 64% · 231 MiB · 1.5 MiB/s*), and you can close the panel and carry on: everyone hears when the floor opens. Whoever added it, or an admin, can stop it with the **⏹️** beside it. A clone that hears nothing from GitHub for 3 minutes is stopped as stalled, one that would have to ask something (an ssh host key, a password) fails straight away and says what to run on the office's machine, and restarting the office doesn't stop one. To add another project, or go to one, walk into the elevator on the north wall and press **E**. Or click the project name in the top-left corner for the list of floors: pick one and you're there in a blink, standing on the same spot in the office. From outside it (the balcony, the street, the garage) it takes you in, and you step out of that floor's elevator. Each floor has its own desks, workers, issues and PR boards, task queue, services and pictures, and its own wall and floor colors, so you always know where you are. You only see and hear the people on your floor. The elevator panel shows how many workers are busy or waiting on someone on each floor, and you get a heads-up when a worker on another floor starts waiting. Every floor is furnished its own way (the elevator and the floor list say how: 🏢 Klassik, 🏭 Industrie-Loft, 🌿 Skandi, 🏛️ Altbau, 🪴 Urban Jungle, 🪩 70er, 🌃 Neon, 🎋 Zen), and an admin can pick another interior for any floor from the list beside it in the elevator ("· auto" is the one its place in the building gives it). An admin can also put the floors in another order: drag a floor by its **⠿** grip to another place in the elevator's list, or move it a floor at a time with **↑** and **↓**. Everyone's building restacks at once. An admin can take a project off the building with the **🗑** beside its floor in the elevator: its workers stop, and everyone on it rides the elevator to another floor. Nothing is deleted. The checkout stays on disk with its workers, queue and pictures, and adding the same repository again moves back into it (as long as the workspace folder hasn't moved since). That goes for the project the office was started in (`agent-office <dir>`) too: it stays off after a restart, and the office keeps its own data in that folder as before.

- **Walk around.** Use WASD, Space to jump, and drag the mouse to orbit the camera. Everyone in the office sees everyone else move in real time.
- **A quiet screen.** The office stays in view: the top bar holds just the floor you're on, **🤖 Workers** and the **☰** menu (or **Tab**). The menu opens every window (issues, PRs, the queue, services, the whiteboard, search, voice, screen sharing, settings) and lists what can show on screen: the workers, who's in the office, spend, Claude limits, the chat and the floor details. Turn each one on or off there, or hide a panel with its **✕**. Pin anything you use a lot to the top bar. The choices are kept in your browser. Chat lines fade after a few seconds; hover the chat or press **T** to read back. What needs you shows up on the top bar by itself: the workers waiting on someone (click to go to them, like **N**), the mute button while you're in voice, **Sharing** while you share your screen, and **⬆️ Update** when there's a new version.
- **Coffee.** Press **E** at the coffee machine in the kitchen for a mug and a minute of quicker walking and higher jumps. A little meter under the project name shows how much buzz is left. Drink a third cup before the last one wears off and you get the jitters for a few seconds.
- **The fridge.** Press **E** at the fridge next to the coffee machine and its door opens on drinks and snacks: a bottle of Helles or Radler (they go to your head like the rooftop bar's beer, the Radler less), cola, Spezi, lemonade, Sprudel, mate or an energy drink (the fizzy ones with caffeine top up the coffee's buzz a little, never to the jitters), and a Brezn, crisps, a chocolate bar, an apple or a Leberkässemmel (eaten in a few bites; the Brezn and the Leberkässemmel soak up a little of the beer, and Sprudel clears your head). Everyone sees what you're holding, and unlike the bar's glasses it comes along to other floors and up to the roof. Had too many and the beer stays in the fridge.
- **Boss office.** Stairs along the back wall climb to a glass-walled office on the loft in the corner. From up there you can look down over every desk and watch your workers go.
- **Ladder and fire poles.** The floors are stacked like a real building, with a ceiling over each. A ladder up the west wall goes through trapdoors in the ceiling and the floor: press **E** to grab it, **W** / **S** to climb, and **E** (or **Space**) to get off. Climb past the ceiling and you come up through the floor of the floor above. To go down in a hurry, walk into the hole round the fire pole (or press **E** at it). You slide down it spinning and come out of the ceiling below. The pole runs the whole way down the building, so a slide takes you one floor down and swings you off beside its hole there, ready to go again. On the bottom floor you land on a mat and a firehouse bell rings, and pressing **E** at the pole there gives you a twirl.
- **Upstairs, over the garage.** The office is the second floor. Its windows are real glass, so you can look out over the street and the city round it. Walk out the EXIT door in the west wall and down the stairs to the street. Underneath is an open garage full of Lambos and Ferraris, which you can jump up onto or drive (see below). The elevator goes down there too (**Garage**, under floor 1 on its panel), stopping at the garage's back wall, and back up from there to your floor or any other.
- **Drive the supercars.** Walk up to one of the Lambos or Ferraris in the garage (or the one parked out front) and press **E** to get behind the wheel. **W** is the gas, **S** brakes and then reverses, **A** and **D** steer, **Space** brakes and **H** honks. Take it out of the garage, round the lots and along the street either way, and on out of town round the scenic loop (see below). Columns, lamp posts, trees and the other cars stop you, and anyone standing in your way gets knocked aside. Everyone on your floor sees the car go by with you in it (the roof comes off, so they can see who's driving), and hears its engine and horn. Press **E** at a car someone else is driving to ride along beside them. **E** gets you out, and the car stays parked wherever you leave it until the office restarts, when every car goes back to its spot. Like the people you see, the cars are your floor's own: the garage under another floor has its cars where people on that floor left them.
- **Flogge's Bulli.** Backed into the garage's east corner, under its own sign: a split-window camper van in teal and cream, surfboard on the roof and FLOGGE on its plates. Only its keyholders take the wheel (every admin until someone is named with `agent-office car keys <name>`); anyone else gets a friendly no and can ride along in the passenger seat. It's slow and soft: it leans out of bends, nods when it brakes, and its horn goes möp möp.
- **A city all round.** The office stands in a city, the same one from every floor, from the street and from the rooftop bar. Streets with sidewalks and zebra crossings run off the office's street between blocks of buildings, lower close by and rising to a skyline of towers further off, with parks, street lamps and trees, benches, bus stops, bike stands, newspaper boxes and advertising pillars pasted with posters of what's on in town. The buildings close by have shops on their ground floor with goods in the windows, striped awnings and signs, lit up at night along with the windows above them and the street lamps, and you can walk into every one of them (see *Shops to walk into* below). The city's own cars drive up and down its streets and stop for you if you step out in front of them. Walk it, or drive the garage's cars through it.
- **Traffic lights and the city bus.** The busier crossings round the office have traffic lights: red, amber and green facing each road coming in (red and amber together just before green, as here), a white stop line, and the red and green man at both ends of each zebra. The two roads take turns, with a moment of red both ways between. The city's cars stop at the line on red and amber and go on green, and the passers-by wait at the curb for the green man; the lamps glow at night. The Flogge Verkehrsbetriebe run three fixed bus lines through town, all past the office: the **7** (*Ring*: Lindenallee, Bowlingcenter, Flogge Office, Kino, Baumarkt), the **12** (*Stadtpark*: Nordstadt, Stadtpark, Kirche, Kino, Soccerhalle, Bowlingcenter, Baumarkt) and the **3** (*Südstadt*: Marktplatz, Südstadt, Casino, Soccerhalle, Gym · Schallwerk, Kino, Tankstelle), two buses on each, every three to four and a half minutes. They're low-floor city buses in their line's color with the line and destination over the windscreen and on the side, keeping to the lights, queueing behind each other at a stop or a red light and letting each other through a crossing, and standing at each stop with their doors open (at the end of a round a little longer, till their next departure). Every stop has its name on the green-and-yellow H sign, the lines that call there, and a departures board counting down the minutes to the next buses (*jetzt* when one's there); where there's no room for a shelter it's just the pole at the curb. People waiting there get on, and now and then someone gets off. Press **E** at an open door to get on, and you're in it as it drives: walk about with **W A S D**, sit down in a free seat with **E** (and up again, or just walk off), press the stop button with **H** (the displays say *HALT*), stamp a ticket in the validator by the doors (**E**), and get off by walking out of an open door (or **E** there). Inside are seats down both sides (the ones by the middle doors red, for those who need them), yellow poles with stop buttons, rails and straps under the ceiling, the pram space, the driver in the cab, two displays with the next stop and the time, the line's stops along the wall with the next one lit, and ads for the town's places over the windows. As it pulls away a gong sounds and a voice calls the next stop (*Nächster Halt: Kino*), at the end of the round *Endhaltestelle*. You hear its diesel and the hiss of its doors. The big map (**J**) shows the lines' routes, their stops and every bus where it is now; the minimap shows the buses round about. Like the passers-by and the lights, the buses run on the office's clock, so everyone sees the same bus at the same place, and the others see you in it, standing, walking about or sitting, right where you are in it. The garage's cars don't have to stop at red, but run one at more than 30 km/h and a speed camera flashes (*Geblitzt!*, on your screen only).
- **People in the streets.** The city's sidewalks are busy: passers-by step out of the shops, walk the blocks at their own pace (alone, two of them chatting, or with a dog on a lead), cross at the zebras while the cars wait for them, sit on the benches, wait at the bus stops, stop to look into a shop window, and go into another shop. Most put up an umbrella when it rains, a few carry shopping bags, and there are fewer about at night. They step aside for you and anyone else down there (or wait a moment when there's no room), glance at you going by, and stumble and look round if you bump into them; you never get stuck on one. Everyone sees the same people at the same places, worked out from the office's clock (nothing goes over the network). Close by you hear their footsteps and the murmur of two talking.
- **Sounds of the city.** Down on the street you hear the city round the office: the road's rumble, louder the closer you are to a street and the more cars there are about, and a whoosh with a drop in pitch as one goes by close; birds in the parks' and the streets' trees by day, crickets under them on warm nights (over 14 °C by the forecast, or May to September without one), neither in the rain; the murmur of people by the shops by day, a cup clinking on a café's terrace; and in the rain, the drumming on the shops' awnings. A little church with a bell tower stands on the park north-west of the office (about 120 m off, the tower toward you): its bell rings morning, noon and evening on the office's sky (at 8, 12 and 18, the hour's count of strokes), about every twenty minutes. Now and then, every ten to twenty minutes on the office's clock, a siren goes by somewhere far off, rising and fading. The bells and the sirens are on the office's clock, so everyone hears them at the same moment. It's all synthesized in the browser and placed where it happens; from the floors and the roof it's fainter, and behind glass (the office, a shop) it's quieter and muffled. It follows the office sounds' volume and mute, goes quiet when the tab's hidden, and **⚙️ → Sound & voice → Stadtgeräusche** turns it off.

- **The minimap.** Bottom right, a round map of where you are that turns with you (straight ahead is up, a little **N** on its rim marks north): the streets and buildings, the office in orange, the casino, the halls, the gym, the golf hole, the Kino, the Tankstelle, the Baumarkt and the church, every shop by its emoji, and the people on your floor as dots in their colors. Once the office is out of its sight an orange arrow at the rim points the way back, and underneath it says where you are (*🥨 Bäckerei*, *🏖️ Sunset Beach*) and how far the office is and which way (*🏢 Büro · 240 m SO*). The mouse wheel over it zooms. **J** (or a click on it) opens the big map: the whole town, the loop round it, the beach, the farm and the lake, north up; drag to look about, the wheel zooms, and the shops' names come in close up. Click a place or a shop and the minimap points you there with a red pin and the distance until you arrive; **Zum Büro** points you home. In the casino, the gym or the halls it shows you at their building. **☰ → Minimap** hides it.
- **The scenic loop.** Both ends of the street run on out of town into a country road, 1.4 km round with the street, that comes back in at the other end. Heading east, it passes Meadowbrook Farm (a red barn, a silo, a windmill, fields of wheat with hay bales and cows in their pasture), turns south into the pines of Whispering Pines and over a creek on a wooden bridge, then west along Summit Pass under a range of snowy mountains, past a lake with a dock, and through the Granite Tunnel under a spur of them. Round the headland it runs north up the coast past Sunset Beach, with palms, umbrellas, a lifeguard tower, a snack shack, a pier out into the sea and sailboats offshore (see *A day at the beach* below), and past the lighthouse on its rocky point, whose beam sweeps round at night, back into town. Drive it either way. A sign at the start of each stretch says where you are, and so does the bar at the bottom of the screen while you drive. The road's edges keep you on it: run wide on a bend and you slide along the edge rather than off it. Out there the haze thins so you can see the mountains and the sea, and the sun's shadows go with you. Anyone can walk out there too. A chequered line across the street in front of the office times laps: cross it, go all the way round (either way) and cross it again, and the bar shows your lap and your best, which is kept in your browser. The upper floors look out over more of it than the bottom one.
- **The cinema.** Behind the office to the north-west (walk west to the next crossing and turn north up that street: it's on your left), the city's cinema has a block of its own: the *Lichtspiele*, with a classic marquee over the entrance whose board says what's on now and next (live from the programme), bulbs round it that chase each other at night, a tall KINO sign up the corner, lit poster cases and a red carpet out to the sidewalk. The glass doors slide open as you walk up. Inside, the foyer: carpet, the programme board between the halls' doors, posters, and the counter, where Heike hands out **popcorn** (fresh from the popping machine on the counter), **nachos** and a **big cola** (**E** there, ✕ top right; all on the house), held like something from the kitchen fridge. **Saal 1** shows real films, back to back, round the clock: the Blender Foundation's open movies (Big Buck Bunny, Sintel, Tears of Steel, Spring, Cosmos Laundromat, Elephants Dream, Sprite Fright, Caminandes) and silent classics in the public domain (A Trip to the Moon, The Great Train Robbery, The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari, Nosferatu). Each streams straight from Wikimedia Commons into your browser (nothing's stored in the office) and runs on the office's clock, so everyone in the hall sees the same moment and coming in late you're where it is now; between films there's a minute's break with the house lights up, the curtain shut and the next film on the screen. As one starts a gong sounds, the lights go down, the curtain opens and the projector's beam reaches over the rows. Every film's credit and licence are on its poster, the screen's card and the plaque under it; **E** at the screen shows the programme with each film's source. The rows rise up the hall: walk up the aisle, sidle along a row and press **E** at a seat to sit (walk off to get up); everyone sees who sits where. You hear the film inside the hall, at your music volume. **Saal 2** is the small one, flat, with armchairs and a starry ceiling: **E** at the lectern by its door puts on a YouTube or Twitch link for everyone in there (guests and party guests too), like the office TV, and **E** at its screen watches it full screen. Each floor's street has its own Saal 2.
- **A day at the beach.** Sunset Beach, on the scenic loop west of town, is for playing (guests and party guests too). The **Kiosk zur Möwe** by the road is open, its shutter propped up as an awning: Uschi stands behind the counter, and **E** there opens the menu (✕ top right): Pommes rot-weiß, Currywurst, a Fischbrötchen, Softeis, an Eis am Stiel, a blue slush, a coconut with an umbrella in it, Eistee and a Strand-Radler, all on the house. She hands it over with a word and a ding of the bell, and you hold it like something from the kitchen fridge (everyone sees it, and it comes along to every floor): ice cream gives you brain freeze, the Currywurst is *mit scharf*, and seagulls are after your fries. **Swim in the sea:** walk in off the sand and it slopes away under you until you're swimming (**W A S D**, **Shift** faster, **Space** splashes), or jump off the end of the jetty or its diving board with a splash. A line of red and white buoys 50 m out marks how far swimmers go; the ladder at the jetty's far end (**E**) climbs back out, or swim in until you can stand. Everyone on the floor sees you swim (breaststroke, or treading water) from where you are, with rings on the water round you. **Jetskis and a motorboat** are moored at the jetty: **E** at one takes the helm (or a free seat; the jetskis take one, the motorboat *Möwe* four), **W A S D** steer on the open water past the shallows (not up the beach, through the pier or onto the lighthouse's rocks), **Space** slows down, **H** sounds the horn, and **E** gets you off onto the jetty when you're alongside it, else over the side into the water (inside the buoys). Their engines and wakes (spray and rings) are there for everyone on the floor, who sees the boats where their drivers are; each floor has its own jetty, and everything's back at its mooring when the office restarts.
- **The Baumarkt.** A block north-east of the office is HAMMER & CO, a DIY store (guests and party guests too): a big orange hall with a blue band and its name in huge letters over the sliding doors under a blue canopy, a pylon sign by the driveway, a car park the garage's cars can drive into from the street, a trolley corral by the doors, a fenced garden centre with plants on tables, trees in pots, sacks of soil on pallets and a glasshouse roof, and a delivery bay with a HAMMER & CO lorry. **Walk in** through the doors (they slide open for you): aisles of high-bay racks stacked with timber, tiles, pipes and paint pots, signs hanging over each aisle, the checkouts with Gabi on the till (**E** there and she rings you up for nothing), and Kalle in his orange apron doing his rounds of the aisles (everyone sees him in the same aisle). **The forklift:** **E** gets on if nobody's driving it; **W** and **S** drive, **A** and **D** steer its rear wheels (its tail swings out), **R** or **Space** raise the forks, **F** or **Shift** lower them, **H** sounds the horn, **E** gets off. Drive the forks into a pallet's pockets and raise them, and it's on; lower them to the floor somewhere else and it's set down there. It beeps backing up, and it stays in the hall and the delivery bay (through the dock's roller door). **The tool wall:** **E** there to take a Bohrmaschine, an Akkuschrauber, a hammer or a Kettensäge to try; click and it whirrs, ratchets, knocks or rasps, for everyone to see and hear; **Q** puts it back (walk out of the hall with it and the gate by the doors beeps, and back on the wall it goes). **The paint shaker:** **E** at the paint counter, pick one of ten colours, and the machine shakes the can loudly for a few seconds before it's yours. **Trolleys:** **E** at one and push it about in front of you; **E** or **Q** lets go, and by the corral it rolls back into its slot. Now and then the PA chimes with an announcement (*Kollege aus der Sanitärabteilung bitte zur Kasse 3*). Each floor has its own, and the office keeps the forklift, the pallets, the trolleys and who holds what; after a restart everything's back in its place. The hall's lights come on after dark, and the signs glow.
- **The petrol station.** A block west of the office, on its own street, stands **FLOGGE OIL** (guests and party guests may use all of it): a canopy over two pump islands with four pumps, LED downlights under it that light the forecourt at night, a price pylon by the street (Super E10, Super E5, Super Plus, Diesel), an air and water post, a vacuum station, a shop with a glass front, and a car wash, with driveways in off the street and out of the wash onto the city's street behind. **Fill up:** drive a garage car (or the Bulli) up beside a pump and stop; **E** from behind the wheel (or standing at the pump) takes the nozzle out of its holster and into the car's filler, and the pump hums and counts the litres and the euros for a few seconds until it clicks off, all on the house (there's no money in the office). **The car wash:** drive in off the forecourt, stop on the yellow marking and press **E** (or **E** at the terminal by the way in): the sign says *Bitte warten*, and the gantry runs along the car spraying water, then pink, yellow and blue foam that settles on it, then its three brushes spin and press against the car, a rinse, and the dryer, about half a minute in all. The car stays put while it runs (the office takes no moves for it); then the light goes green, the sign says *Ausfahrt frei*, and you drive out the north door with the paint gleaming and glinting for ten minutes. Walk in while it sprays and you get soaked. **The shop:** the sliding door opens as you come up; **E** at the counter and Jacqueline scans and hands over a coffee to go (the kitchen machine's buzz), a Schokoriegel, Chips, an Energy-Drink, a Bockwurst or the paper, held like something from the kitchen fridge. Everyone on the floor sees the hose go in, the pump count, the wash's programme and the shiny car; each floor has its own, and the office keeps its clock.
- **A tower that grows with your projects.** Every floor is a storey of the same building, so from outside it's as tall as you have projects: add enough of them and it's a skyscraper, a balcony off every floor, windows lit here and there at night and a cornice round the top. No two storeys are cut alike: each floor's balcony hangs somewhere else than the one below's (another stretch of the street side, or round the side of the building off the east or west wall), some floors have a second, smaller one on another side, the windows make way for their doors, the desk pods sit (and now and then turn) their own way, and one wall inside is painted a color of its own. Outside, it's a building for creatives: anthracite walls with murals right across the back and sides, rainbow fins down the street side that light up at night, a band round every storey in the color of how it's furnished (lit underneath at night), FLOGGE OFFICE in lit letters on the roof's parapet (stand right at them up on the roof terrace), a neon CREATE blade on the corner, a giant light bulb, a paper plane, a pencil stuck in the wall and paint dripping off the top. Inside, each storey is furnished its own way too: the bottom floor as the office always was, the floors above taking turns with the other interiors, each with its own floor, ceiling, walls, lamps, rugs, furniture colors and decor (a loft's brick, ducts and caged bulbs; Altbau parquet, wainscoting, stucco and chandeliers; hanging plants and a moss wall; a disco ball and lava lamps; neon strips and signs; tatami, shoji and paper lanterns). Up on a higher floor the street is that many storeys further down. Only the bottom floor has the EXIT door and the stairs down; the floors above it have wall there, so take the elevator down to the garage to leave.
- **Shops to walk into.** Every shop in town is open: walk in through its door (no loading, it's the same street) and you're in its shop, seen through its windows by everyone outside, lit warm at night. There are twenty-two kinds, spread all over town (the ones nearest the office are one of each): a Bäckerei, a café, a pizzeria, an Apotheke, a florist, a bookshop, a kiosk, a bar, a Späti, a barber (Friseur), a tattoo and piercing studio, a Döner, a toy shop (Spielzeug), a record shop (Platten), a boutique (Klamotten), an optician (Optik), an arcade (Spielhalle), a post office (Post), an ice cream parlour (Eisdiele), a sushi bar, a butcher (Metzgerei) and a supermarket. Each has its counter with someone behind it who turns to look at you, and its own things: shelves of goods, café tables, the bar's bottles, the pharmacy's green cross, buckets of flowers, bookshelves, drinks fridges, the barber's chairs and mirrors, the tattoo studio's flash on the walls, the Döner's turning spit and its menu board, a toy train going round its table and teddies in the window, crates of records and a turntable. **E** at a counter opens its menu (✕ top right, all on the house): what you're handed is in your hand for everyone to see and comes along to every floor, like the fridge's things. The bar's Zwickl, Spritz, Gin Tonic, Negroni and Obstler go to your head like the rooftop bar's; the Apotheke's headache pill clears it a good deal quicker; the café's coffees give you the kitchen machine's buzz; the Döner man asks *mit alles? scharf?*, and scharf is scharf. At the **Friseur**, **E** at a chair sits you down for a new hair style, hair colour and beard; at the **tattoo studio**, a tattoo (anchor, rose, *Mama* heart, skull, swallow, tribal band, a little robot…) on your forearm, upper arm, neck or hand, a piercing (ear lobes, helix rings, nose, septum, eyebrow, lip; silver or gold), or the laser to take one off. Your new look is saved and everyone sees it, like a change in the character window. The **boutique** has rails and racks of clothes, mannequins in the window, a tall mirror and changing cubicles: step into one and its curtain draws shut (everyone sees it shut while someone's in there). **E** at a rack or in a cubicle opens the clothes window: a T-shirt, hoodie, shirt, blazer, leather jacket or dress, its colour (your shirt colour), the trousers' colour, and a cap, beanie, hat or sun hat. The **optician** (Optik) has a wall of glasses, a table of frames, an eye chart and a mirror: **E** to try on round, square, nerd, aviator or cat-eye frames with clear, dark or mirrored lenses (aviators come dark). Both go on at once in the mirror, **Nehm ich!** keeps them, and the character window has the same rows. The **toy shop**'s toys are for playing with: click (or **E** with nothing else in reach) and everyone on your floor sees and hears it: the yo-yo runs down its string, soap bubbles float off and pop, the duck squeaks, the paper plane glides off and lands, the water pistol squirts (whoever it hits gets a little splash, nothing more), the teddy gets a hug. At the **record shop**, **E** at a crate digs through the records (a dozen of them, each with its own sleeve), and **E** at the listening station puts the headphones on: only you hear the record, played by the jukebox's own synthesizer with its own melody. A book from the bookshop (or the kiosk's paper) opens to read; **E** opens it again while it's in your hand. In the **Spielhalle** (neon outside, dark and neon inside) the office's own arcade cabinets stand in a row: **E** plays BLOCKFALL just for you. Its **claw machine** has a pile of plush toys (a cat, a bunny, a dino, a panda, an octopus): **E**, steer the claw with the arrows or **W A S D** before the clock runs out and drop it with **Space**; the office decides whether it holds (dead centre most of the time, near the edge hardly ever), everyone near sees the claw move and come back up, and a plush that makes it down the chute is in your hand for everyone to see. Its **photo booth**: step in with **E**, the curtain closes, and before each of four shots you pick a pose (the emotes, which your character plays in the shop for everyone too); the countdown, the flash, and out comes a black-and-white strip of four to download as a PNG, and to carry about.
