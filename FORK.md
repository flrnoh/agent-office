# flrnoh/agent-office: Florian's own office

This repository is a fork of [AgentSystemLabs/agent-office](https://github.com/AgentSystemLabs/agent-office) (webdevcody's). It is Florian's own version: changed to his liking, and still taking in what the upstream community builds. Where this file and `CLAUDE.md` disagree, this file wins.

## How the two stay together

- **`main` here is Florian's office.** It runs on his Mac as the launch agent `com.flrnoh.agent-office`, public at https://buero.florian-obermeier.com (Cloudflare tunnel).
- **Own changes** go in as a branch and a pull request into this fork's `main`, as usual. Squash-merging those is fine. Here the pull requests to merge are Florian's and his workers' (the "only webdevcody's" rule in `CLAUDE.md` is upstream's).
- **Upstream's changes** come in through a sync pull request (branch `sync/upstream-<sha>`). The `Upstream sync` workflow (`.github/workflows/upstream-sync.yml`) offers one every morning (daily, 05:00 UTC), or on demand from the Actions tab:
  - ✅ merges cleanly, builds, tests pass: ready to merge.
  - ⚠️ merges cleanly, but the build or tests fail: usually something new to sort for guests (below).
  - Conflicts: an issue lists the files, to resolve by hand, keeping both sides.
- **Merge sync pull requests with "Create a merge commit", never squash or rebase.** Only a merge commit tells git that upstream's commits are in; after a squash the next sync brings all of them again, conflicting everywhere. (This overrides the squash rule in `CLAUDE.md` for sync pull requests.)
- **After merging anything**, bring the Mac's office up to date and restart it:
  ```bash
  ~/cloude_code/agent-office/bin/update-office.sh
  ```
- **Never push to or open pull requests on upstream.** The `upstream` remote's push URL is disabled on purpose; nothing from this fork goes back.
- **No other ties to upstream.** The only link is reading its public repository for the sync. Upstream's `Release` workflow is switched off in this repository's Actions settings (it would publish releases here on every merge); its file stays, so syncs don't conflict over it. The office itself doesn't call upstream at runtime: the built-in updater is off (no `AGENT_OFFICE_SELF_UPDATE`) and would only follow this fork, and the image proxy names this fork in its user agent. Upstream's install scripts and hosting docs (`install.sh`, `deploy/`, `docs/*`) still point at upstream: they're not used here, and are left alone so syncs stay conflict-free.

## Keeping conflicts rare

- Put new things in **files of their own** (like `src/server/guests.ts`) and touch upstream's files only for the few lines that hook them in.
- Keep those hook lines short and recognisable, so a conflict shows at a glance what to keep.
- List every change of this fork below, with the upstream files it touches.

Upstream split its hub files (`server.ts`, `main.ts`, `protocol.ts`, `sound.ts`, `state.ts`) into registries and feature folders; build on those:

- **Client:** a new fork thing is a feature, a folder under `src/client/features/<name>/` with an `install…` function, called once from `src/client/main.ts` (the fork's block there) and listed in `core/parts.ts` when other parts need it. A kind of thing to use is declared (`declare module '…/world/types' { interface InteractKinds … }`) and defined (`ctx.interactions.define`) in the same feature file; kinds are plain lowercase (`[a-z]+`, upstream's registry test), like `casinotable`, `gymstation`, `soccerpitch`. Fixtures on the office floor go in `src/client/world/office/fork.ts`, listed in `world/office/build.ts`.
- **Server:** messages go in `src/shared/protocol/fork.ts` (`ForkClientMsg`/`ForkServerMsg`) and are handled in `src/server/ws/handlers/fork.ts` (`forkHandlers`; letting go on leaving or closing in `forkHooks`); what they keep lives on the `Fork` context in `src/server/fork/office.ts`; roles in `src/server/fork/roles.ts`; HTTP routes in `src/server/http/routes/fork.ts`.
- **Sound:** a module per feature in `src/client/sound/` (or the feature's own `sound.ts`), with a facade method in the fork's section of `sound/index.ts`.
- Upstream files only get hook lines, marked `// flrnoh fork` or `// fork:`. `git diff upstream/main -- <path>` shows exactly the fork's lines in one.

### Padel

Two padel courts in the padel hall (the building, the way in, the stand, the gallery and its café are the hall's own, see `src/shared/hall.ts`): blue courts with white lines, the net on its posts, glass back walls (3 m) with the wire fence on top to 4 m, side glass 2 m out from each back wall and fence between, an opening in each side on either side of the net, lights overhead, and a scoreboard hanging over each court's north end for the gallery (both faces). **E** at an opening opens a chooser (✕ top right): a place (south or north end, right or left; 1–4) or 👀 Watch. It's always two against two: the computer plays every place nobody has, so one person plays with a computer partner against two computer players, and two can play each other (a computer partner each) or together. Guests and party guests play too.

Playing, the camera glides in behind your player; they run for the ball by themselves (the chooser's "run for the ball" box; **WASD** steers while held), the mouse aims (a ring on the court), click or **Space** swings (the racket meets the ball as it comes past, and how well the swing's timed decides how hard and true it goes: early or late floats, strays, and now and then finds the net or their glass on the full), **Shift** lobs, a high ball near the net is smashed. ✕ or **Esc** steps off through the nearest opening, back to mouse-look.

Rules, simplified but recognisable: the serve is underhand after a bounce, diagonally into the box across (from the right when the game's points add up even), two tries; the ball must bounce once in the other half before it touches that half's walls; after the bounce it may come off the glass (any number of times) and be played, volleys are fine except on the return of serve; a second bounce, the wire fence, or out through an opening or over the top ends the point (for the hitter after the bounce, against them before it); into the net and down on your own side is the other side's. 15, 30, 40, game, a **golden point** at 40–40, first to **four games** (no sets or tie-break: four computer players take 5–8 minutes, `tests/padel.test.ts` checks it). The teams serve a game each in turn, the players within a team by turns. A new match starts by itself 8 s after one ends.

It runs like the table games on the roof: the first person on a court hosts it (their page runs the game and the computer players and sends a snapshot about 25 times a second through the office to everyone in the hall); the others on the court send their moves (how they move, a swing with its aim) to the host. The office checks who may send what (only inside the hall, only the court's host snapshots, only its other players move, a rate limit each, plain numbers of a bounded size), relays, and keeps who's on which place, the games and the last snapshot for whoever comes in later. When the host leaves (steps off, leaves the hall, closes the page) the next person on the court takes over from the last snapshot; someone joining mid-match takes a computer player's place and the match goes on; the last one off frees the court.

### TURN for voice from outside

Voice and screen sharing connect browsers directly (WebRTC). With only STUN that fails for people outside the Mac's network behind strict routers, office networks or mobile data: they are "in voice" and their mic lights up, but nothing arrives. With a Cloudflare TURN key the office fetches short-lived TURN credentials (48 h, refreshed every 12 h, retried after 5 min on failure) and hands them to every page in the welcome's `ice`, after the office's own STUN servers. Without a key nothing changes. Port-53 URLs are dropped (browsers block them).

- The key: `<office>/.agent-office/turn.json` = `{"keyId": "...", "apiToken": "..."}` (chmod 600), or the environment's `CF_TURN_KEY_ID` / `CF_TURN_API_TOKEN`. Read at start: restart the office after adding it.

### The soccer hall

Across the street between the golf hole and the padel hall (x 2.5..21, z 36..72, where the last neighbour across the street stood; ≥ 1 m from the green and its fringe, 1 m from the padel hall), an indoor small-field soccer hall: a long dark green hall with a band of milky polycarbonate glazing under the eaves (lit at night), white SOCCER ARENA letters with a ball, glass doors under a steel canopy (a green light strip along its edge, downlights under it) on the north (street) face, the club's red and blue FLOGGE FC banners, uplights washing the facade at night, a match poster by the doors, a little practice goal on the lawn, and the SCENIC LOOP billboard, which moved up onto its roof. **E** at the doors goes in; **E** at the doors inside comes back out onto the street of the floor you came from. Inside is a place of its own like the casino and the padel hall (`SOCCER = '@soccer'` is your `floor` in there; interior coordinates, the pitch centred on 0): a 27 × 14 m pitch of artificial turf with mown stripes and crisp white lines (halfway line, centre circle, futsal-style penalty areas and spots, corner arcs) and the FLOGGE FC crest in the centre circle, 1 m boards all round (a rubber kick plate, LED panels on both faces, a padded top) with safety nets above (4.5 m behind the goals, 3 m along the sides), two 3 × 2 m goals (round white posts and bar, a frame back to a weighted base, nets that sag and billow out when the ball goes in), dugouts (red north, blue south of the halfway line) with curved acrylic roofs, bucket seats, bottle crates, cones and a ball bag along the west wall, the players' tunnel (SPIELERTUNNEL) between them and a locker room (KABINE) behind glass further north, a three-row stand behind each goal (blue end south, red end north west of the doors) and a gallery over the east walkway, padded walls with ribbed cladding and a band of windows (daylight, or the night outside), steel trusses with red and blue banners and rows of floodlights under the roof, and a big scoreboard over each end (RED n : m BLUE, the clock, what's going on; it flashes the scoring team's colour for a goal).

The hall's atmosphere (`world/soccer/look.ts`, the rules in `matchday.ts`): the stands fill with instanced fans (178 seats, red at the north end and the gallery's north half, blue at the south, neutral between; more of them with more people in the hall, always a good crowd during a match, nearly empty when nobody's in). They bob and chat, clap a kick-off and along with the chants, get half up with their hands up for a near miss (off the post or the bar, or hard into the end boards just wide), and jump with arms and scarves up when their team scores (the other end sits it out). The LED boards roll through friendly fake ads (Kultur am Regen, Bus kommt gleich, Bibelstunde Cocktails, Signal & Stille, Casino gegenüber, Padel Hall, …) and strobe GOAL! in the scorers' colour for a goal. The stadium announcer calls out the kick-off (ANPFIFF!), goals (⚽ TOOOR! Ann trifft – Rot 2:1 Blau, own goals too) and the final whistle in a big callout with an air horn. The floodlights flare as a match starts and dim a little between matches; at night the windows go dark and their pools on the pitch show more. Sounds (effects volume, only in the hall): a crowd murmur that swells with the crowd and the play, the roar of a goal, the "oooh", applause, rhythmic clapping now and then in play. All of it costs well under a millisecond a frame (one draw call per body part for the whole crowd, one mesh for every LED panel, the hall's plain parts merged into one).

Playing: **E** at the boards on the halfway line (either side) puts you in the team with fewer players (on a tie the one behind, else red; five a side at most), on the pitch in your half, with a bib and a ring under your feet in the team's colour for everyone to see; **E** there again leaves the pitch (so do leaving the hall and disconnecting; a reconnect joins again). The ball is the office's: `server/soccer/` runs the physics (`shared/soccer-ball.ts`) in fixed 1/60 s steps (`SIM_DT`, 30 ticks a second), tuned like a heavy futsal ball: rolling friction 1.5 m/s² plus drag 0.4/s (a 6 m/s pass dies after ~6 m, 7 m/s ~7.6 m), a low bounce (0.32; dropped from 2 m it comes up ~0.2 m, and the turf takes 15% of its pace on each bounce), air drag, the boards (restitution 0.62; the nets above them hold a high ball in, so it never leaves the pitch), the posts and the bar (0.55), the net nearly dead, and a ball lying dead against the boards or in a corner rolls gently back out. A goal is the whole ball over the line between the posts under the bar. Players' positions are their ordinary `move` messages; their speed comes from the last two, the way they face from `rotY`. Close control (`touchBall`): run onto the ball (its middle within 1 m of yours, low, slower than 15 m/s relative to you) and it's yours; the first touch cushions it (80% of its speed relative to you taken off). While you have it, it's kept on a soft spring 0.75 m ahead of your feet the way you run (a little lag on a turn; at a sprint up to 1.35 m, the knock-on), and standing it's trapped 0.55 m in front of you; you keep it within 1.8 m. An opponent clearly better placed (nearer, running at it) takes it off you; a close call pops it loose sideways, and whoever lost it waits 350 ms. A ball too fast or too high to take comes off players' bodies (they're 1.8 m tall, so a lob goes over). Kicking (`client/soccer/controls.ts`, `passKick`, `shotKick`): a tap of the left mouse button or Space passes (the teammate best in line with where you look, within ±29° and 1.5–22 m, led if they run, paced to arrive at ~3 m/s; with nobody there, ~8 m along your aim); holding it charges a shot (full after 0.9 s, at least 30% power) that goes along the crosshair, rising as far as you look up (up to 0.45 rad; first person); the right button (or C) does the same through the air (a lob that comes down just short of the teammate, or a chip). A kick let go of up to 0.2 s before the ball's in reach (1.5 m) waits for it, and one up to 0.2 s after it was still goes. On the page, the aim shows as a ring round the crosshair with the power meter under it, the receiver's name, and a line on the floor from the ball the way it'll go. The office takes a kick (`soccer.kick {power, dir, loft, lift}`) only from a player whose team may touch the ball, once per 250 ms, when the ball was within 1.5 + 0.35 m of where the office has them at some moment in the last 350 ms (or they're dribbling it), all numbers clamped. The office sends the ball about 15 times a second while it moves (at once after a kick, a hit or a change of who has it), with its physics step `k` and who has it `c`. Your own kick goes at once on your page (the same `kick` and the same steps as the office, so the two agree to the last digit; once the office has had the kick its snapshots, run on by the round trip, nudge it), and so does taking and dribbling the ball (it stays at your feet as you run; the office taking it off you ends that). Everyone else's ball is played back 100 ms behind the office's clock, on a curve between the snapshots either side, run on for up to 250 ms when one's late; switching between the two melts away over a few frames. The match (`server/soccer/match.ts`, on its own clock for the tests): it starts once both teams have a player; a 2 s kick-off freeze with the ball on the centre spot, then the kicking-off team touches it first (for up to 3 s); 5 minutes of playing time (the clock stops for goals and kick-offs) or first to 5 goals; after a goal a 3 s celebration (toast "⚽ GOAL! Red (Ann) · Red 1:0 Blue", confetti in the goal, the cheer and the whistle), then the team that conceded kicks off; the final whistle, the result as a toast, 10 s, a new match. A team left empty pauses it (clock stopped, score kept) and it kicks off again once both have players; nobody left on the pitch resets it. Meanwhile (waiting or paused) the ball's free to kick about, and practice goals just bring it back to the centre. Guests and party guests play too.

Stats, replays and the kit: the match keeps its statistics (`server/soccer/stats.ts`, `MatchStats`, the match's `stats`): every touch in play (a kick, or running into the ball) in order, by who someone is (`account:<id>` or `name:<name>`, like the casino's chips). A goal gets the scorer (the last touch, if it's their team's; else an own goal, the toucher's), the assist (the last touch before the scorer's by someone else, if it's a teammate's and at most `ASSIST_MS` = 6 s before the goal) and the minute; a kick is a shot when it's hard enough, from near enough, and the physics run on (`runOn`) takes it to the other goal's end line near the posts, on target when it'd go in (a goal always counts as one); a pass is completed when a kick's next touch is a teammate's; possession is the playing time each team had the last touch; a save is a shot on target whose next touch is a defender's near their own goal line. At the final whistle the man of the match (`shared/soccer-stats.ts` `pickMvp`: goals ×3, assists ×2, shots on target and saves ×1; ties to more goals, then the winners) and everyone's match go on the all-time leaderboard (`server/soccer/records.ts`, `SoccerRecords`: matches, wins, goals, assists, MVP awards, and the shirt number they keep, 1–99, from their key's hash or the next free one; `<dataDir>/soccer.json`, 0600, written beside it and moved over it). The `soccer` state carries `stats` and `leaders` (the top five) and each player's `number`; no new messages. The scoreboards (`client/soccer/scoreboard.ts`, drawn into the interior's scoreboard canvas) show RED n : m BLUE, the clock, the phase, the scorers under each team ("⚽ Ann 3'"), shots and a possession bar, and at full time the result, the MVP and the two teams side by side; a Hall of Fame board on the north wall west of the scoreboard shows the top five. On the page (`client/soccer/show.ts`, `SoccerShow`, made by `SoccerPlace`): a line under the score bar (the last goal, Tab), the stats panel on **Tab** (the match, the players, the leaderboard; ✕, Tab or Esc closes it), the kit (`client/soccer/kit.ts`: a team-coloured shirt over body and arms with the short name and number on the back and the number on the chest, shorts and socks; the ring under the feet stays `SoccerPlace`'s) and the moves laid over each body after `Person.update` (a kick's leg swing from the ball's `by`, the scorer's celebration, `pickCelebration`: arms up, knee slide, aeroplane or jumping, teammates within 4.5 m put a hand up, and a dive, `diveSide`, for someone in their own penalty area when a fast ball will pass them 0.4–2.2 m to the side). The instant replay (`client/soccer/replay.ts`): each page records what it shows (the ball, and where everyone on the pitch stood and faced, 7 s back, and kicks); `REPLAY.startAfterMs` (1.8 s) after a goal it cuts the 3.2 s before it and 0.2 s after and plays it back on everyone's screen from a TV camera swinging round the ball (letterbox bars, a REPLAY badge, the last 0.8 s at half speed, then SLOW-MO), putting the bodies and the ball where they were; **Space** or **Esc** skips it, and a kickoff ends it. `GOAL_MS` is 6.5 s so the celebration and the replay fit before the kickoff (a test checks).

Slide tackles and fouls (`shared/soccer-tackle.ts` the rules, `server/soccer/tackle.ts` the office's side, `client/soccer/tackle.ts` the page's): **Q** (or **Ctrl**; while you play, Q's own jobs, putting back a card or dropping the basketball, don't apply on the pitch, and a bare Ctrl does nothing else in the 3D view) slides you the way you face. The slide's curve is the same everywhere (`SLIDE`: 3.5 m in 450 ms on an ease-out with exponent 1.6, so it leaves at ~12 m/s and stops dead; then 400 ms lying and 400 ms getting up, `SLIDE_MS` 1250; it stops `SLIDE_EDGE` 0.4 m inside the boards: `makeSlide`, `slideAt`). The page sends `soccer.slide {dir, x, z}` and at once drives you along the curve itself (`player.rig`, so its `move`s are where the office has you; keys and kicks do nothing until you're up; the view sinks up to 0.95 m in first person, 0.55 m in third, `player.eyeDrop`). The office checks it (on a team, on the pitch, one per `cooldownMs` 2 s, not sliding already, not at a set piece, a goal or full time, nor in the other team's kick-off: `slideRefused`; it takes the page's start when it's within `SLIDE_TRUST` 1.5 m of where it has you), rounds the curve to the millimetre and sends it to everyone (`soccer.slide {id, x, z, dir, d}`; a refusal only to you, at most every 600 ms, and your page stands you up again). Every page moves that body along the same curve and poses it (`Person.rig()`, after the kit's moves: down on the hip leaning back, the leading leg out, a hand behind) with turf flying from the boot. Every tick, over the slide's first `activeMs` 350 ms, the office looks for contacts on a fixed 1/240 s grid (`firstContact`): the ball (its middle within `BALL_HIT_R` 0.5 m of a foot zone `FOOT_AHEAD` 0.75 m ahead of the body, under 0.5 m high, and only while the slider's team may touch it) or an opponent's legs (the sliding leg, a segment `LEG_LEN` 1 m out along the slide, within `LEGS_R` 0.4 m of where they stand). Whichever comes first counts; both in the same instant is the ball's. The ball: poked on along the slide (`POKE_V` 7 m/s, or the slide's speed + 1.5), or, when the slide is slower than `WIN_V` 5 m/s by then, rolled on to stop 0.6 m past where the slider ends up (`pokeOf`, won); whoever had it, or stood within 1.8 m of it, can neither take nor block it for 600 ms, and an opponent's ball is a tackle won (`tackle` event, a short cheer). The legs, in a match in play (no fouls in practice): a foul (`foul` event: who, on whom, penalty, card), the match goes to `freekick` or `penalty` (`SoccerMatch.foul`, the clock stopped), the ball dead still at `foulSpot` (where the fouled player stood, kept `SPOT_EDGE` 2 m off the boards), or on the penalty spot (6 m out) when the foul was in the fouler's own penalty area (`inPenaltyArea`: within 5 m of the goal line between the posts). From `SETUP_MS` 0.8 s on, the fouled team's first kick (`setPieceKick`; at a penalty only the taker's, the fouled player) restarts play (`restart`); nobody runs the ball along meanwhile, and after `SET_PIECE_MS` 8 s it goes on by itself ("⏱️ … not taken: play on"). The pages keep the fouling team `FREEKICK_RING` 3 m off the ball for `RING_MS` 3 s, and at a penalty everyone but the taker and the keeper `PENALTY_RING` 4 m (`ringPush`; a yellow ring on the floor shows it); they put the taker `TAKER_BACK` 1.4 m behind the spot and the keeper (the defenders' player nearest their goal, picked by the office) on the line. The fouled player goes down for `FALL_MS` 1.5 s (on their own page they can't move meanwhile). Fouls count per player per match (`MatchStats.foul`): the second is a yellow card, the third a red one (`cardFor`), which sends the player off the pitch and keeps them off for `RED_OUT_MS` 60 s. `SoccerPlayer.card` carries it: 🟨/🟥 by the name in the **Tab** stats, the bookings on the scoreboards (drawn cards: not every canvas has colour emoji). Stats: tackles won, fouls and fouls suffered per player, tackles and fouls per team (in Tab, and two more rows at full time); the man of the match gets a point per tackle won (`mvpScore`). The atmosphere: the referee's double whistle (`sound.soccer('foul')`), the stands whistle and boo (crowd act `boo`, `soccerCrowd('boo')`), the announcer calls FOUL! (FREISTOSS für Rot) or ELFMETER!, and 1.7 s later the card (🟨 Gelbe Karte – Bob, `cardCallout`); a clean tackle gets applause and a short cheer from the tackler's end.
